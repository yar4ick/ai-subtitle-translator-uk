- C: every character who speaks or is spoken to or about more than once. code: one or two Cyrillic letters, unique. The Ukrainian name in the form used when speaking to the character is not needed; give the base form.
- Ukrainian names by current orthography and by the rules of the name's own language (Martinez → Мартінес, Jean → Жан, Mathilda → Матильда); keep the established Ukrainian form if the film has one (UKRAINIAN WIKIPEDIA and UKRAINIAN SPELLINGS, when given, show it).
- P: how the first character addresses the second; one line per direction when they differ (a child to an adult, a subordinate to a boss). Note when it changes, e.g. «спершу ви, потім ти».
- T: invented words, slang, places, organisations, nicknames, recurring catchphrases, forms of address worth fixing. Pick one rendering each.
- Slang and forms of address by meaning, with a Ukrainian word of the same register (choom → друже, nova → круто, gonk → бовдур); transliterate names of people and places, brands and invented technical terms (Night City → Найт-Сіті, Sandevistan → Сандевістан), unless they have an established Ukrainian form.
- A short name, nickname or surname may belong to a character you already have (Stan = Norman Stansfield): add it to that character's role, not as a new C line, unless the lines show two different people.
- Gender: when it is a guess, write `ч?` or `ж?`. A character who names themselves ("names themselves" in CANDIDATES) belongs in CAST even if mentioned once.
- If you are not sure of a term, prefer plain transliteration over a coined word.
- Put `?` at the end of any line you are not sure of. Do not invent characters.
